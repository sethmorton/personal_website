# Project Learnings

- Keep the horn image as the hero for “The Shape of Inference” unless the user explicitly requests replacing it; a request for a new visualization does not authorize replacing the hero.
- Give the settling visualization fast, sweeping waves and moving balls while preserving the site's paper, indigo, and gold palette.
- For the eyepiece teaser, build co-construction from the horn's geometry: continuous centered handoffs, recognizable fragments that alter earlier paths, and a shared form; edit the teaser, not the separate settling visualization.
- Source real mid-century research imagery for the eyepiece opener—Bell Labs teams, oscilloscopes, analog computers, waveforms and experimental apparatus—and retain dates and credits with the assets.
- Prove teaser transitions in rough motion before polishing: keep a recognizable element moving across each handoff, avoid a card collage over the horn or a plunge into black, and make the final form's relationship to its source fragments readable.
- A horn-to-curve morph is insufficient continuity: preserve the horn's recognizable structure through the handoff and establish why each new form appears; avoid radial connector lines with photos or words attached as labels.

- Keep the personal homepage within 100vh on normal desktop windows, with softened Prussian blue accents, botanical artwork without planets or orbits, and subtle reduced-motion-aware animation; use Georgia for titles, Literata for the bio, and Source Sans 3 for summaries/navigation; avoid all-serif styling, spaced uppercase monospace labels, and Archivo footer links.
- For the approved eyepiece direction, transform the horn itself as one connected shell; inspect every exported frame in sequence and document transition defects before delivering another MP4.

- Center the personal homepage as one compact reading column; keep the illustration secondary, avoid table-like rules between essays, and limit essay hover to title color and small arrow movement; do not tint whole rows or shift their text.

- Use `src/lib/styles/theme.css` for shared design values and `ContentLink.svelte` for essay/project entries; follow `DESIGN.md` instead of adding page-local palette or font literals.

- Preserve the eyepiece’s archival granularity through the horn transformation by attaching photographic texture to the deforming surface; background grain alone does not preserve the material.

- Match eyepiece titles to the blog’s Georgia heading and Literata body fonts; keep the final horn’s throat and aperture legible, avoid shell-like scallops, and use technical diagrams only to explain the essay’s actual argument.

- In the teaser’s state-vector beat, show only the “Persistent state” title above the animation; keep explanatory captions and schematic labels in the accompanying notes.

- Carry visible motion from the archival montage through the horn handoff; overlap the photographic reveal, camera move, and first deformation so the horn never reads as a held still before restarting.

- Keep the eyepiece ending simple: use continuous motion of the horn itself for intensity while keeping its entire throat and aperture inside both wide and square frames; do not surround it with extra archival panels or restore the generic closing sentence.

- Give the archival horn a readable moving recognition beat before it transforms; use a gentle push-in instead of rushing directly into the morph.

- Review eyepiece transitions for silhouette continuity, not just continuous pixels: retain the photographed horn’s large flare and support wheel through the handoff; composite an opaque instrument over a separate fading background to avoid a translucent cutout stage.

- In associative-state figures, include sparse long-range connections across branches or regions; their forces must participate in the dynamics, not merely decorate a nearest-neighbor web.

- Keep the essay hierarchy about co-construction and the dense springs about growing computation; distinguish them through motion and simple counts, not an unexplained cell grid.
- Use the original stacked-surface Three.js helpers scene for the HAWF clip and preserve the author’s chosen stream variant for causal generation.

- Preserve the approved granular triangular mesh and three-agent composition for passing notes; avoid replacing it with generic clustered node diagrams. Use Extropic references for simplicity and causal motion, not their palette or visual styling.

- Show only one selected animation per passage in both development and production, with short captions and no semicolons or simulation disclaimers. Keep Stream selected for causal generation.
- In passing notes, recipients must visibly rebuild different structures from the note within their own contexts, rather than reproduce the sender’s unchanged mesh.

- Count cumulative relationship checks across repeated rounds in the dense-springs figure, not just connections at the current size.
- Keep the HAWF clip to one ball on the stacked fields, with a subtle paper-toned background and no bounding box or floor grid.
- Label only a few essay blocks, and give passing-notes reconstructions time to complete while the shared mesh visibly deforms.

- Exclude .svelte-kit and other local build caches explicitly in .vercelignore before CLI deployments, and check upload size against the production source bundle.

- Use real section headings in essay Markdown so subtitles inherit the blog’s heading size rather than body-sized bold paragraph text.
